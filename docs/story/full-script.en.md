# FINAL ORBIT · Full campaign script

See the [campaign outline](main-story.md) for roles and unlock conditions and the [player guide](story-explainer.en.md) for onboarding and terms. Scene numbers follow the original proposal. The briefings, workbench, events, and results carry these scenes. The bilingual dictionaries contain the shorter lines used by the game.

## Prologue · The lost signal

### P01 | Ten minutes to launch

Mission control shows “ORBITAL DEMONSTRATION 01” and an avionics warning.

**Elena** — “The warning keeps coming and going. We haven’t found the cause. I recommend holding the countdown.”

**Alex** — “Investors need to see a result today. If we slip, we could lose the next payment.”

The authorization goes into the record with the player’s signature. The first mission brief lets players expand and read the memory.

### P02–P03 | Loss of contact and investigation

The ground team initially receives telemetry after liftoff. The signal degrades and disappears. The mission fails and investors pull out. The investigation establishes that a warning was still open before launch. The available evidence cannot establish the cause of the loss. Two years later, the company is about to close.

## Chapter 1 · First Light

### 101–103 | The lab and the urgent commission

**Alex** — “One of the government’s contractors missed a delivery. Samuel Reed from the space program can arrange a ride to orbit if we take over.”

**Samuel** — “We can take on one of these two commissions. Pick a field team to support and bring back usable data by the end of first contact.”

The player selects the flood access map or the bay discharge survey. The selected commission supplies the instrument, delivery threshold, and use of the data.

**Elena** — “I’m coming back for this one. Before we release the satellite, I want a report for the configuration we actually built.”

### 104–105 | Spacecraft design and flight release

The configuration cards compare launch and observation options. A pointing reserve can allow an extra observation. An auxiliary sensor can recover obscured data and adds an interface to integrate. If testing finds a fault in that interface, show the design correction, rework, removal, and retest options that apply to this run.

**Elena** — “Let’s check the report against the vehicle that will fly. The release decision goes in the mission record.”

A day-in-the-life test runs separation, deployment, and first transmission on the ground. The game checks the selected launch’s interface requirements. [NASA CubeSat 101, §6.9.1](https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/nasa_csli_cubesat_101_508.pdf#page=67)

### 106–108 | First contact and the next proposal

The ground station receives health telemetry and the primary observations. Extra targets require a completed base delivery, spare downlink capacity, and an opportunity to observe. The report shows what the receiving team could do with this run’s observations.

- Delivery complete. **Samuel** — “The field team has the data it needed. Your delivery is on record, and an orbital sponsor has asked for your next proposal.”
- Delivery short. **Elena** — “The field team is still waiting for usable data. Let’s follow the integration and ground-pass records to see where the delivery fell short.”
- Flight held. The report identifies where work stopped. Elena keeps the report so the player can address the cause on a replay.

## Chapter 2 · Second Attempt

### 201–202 | Orbital demonstration and the second payment

**Alex** — “A sponsor saw our satellite delivery and wants us to demonstrate our own launcher again. We owe them proof that the payload reached the orbit in the contract.”

The player compares contract orbits, upper stages, diagnostics, adapters, and ground stations. The CubeSat integration record can earn the team a lab slot. When the sponsor cuts the second payment, the event shows the funds left and the cost of completing each available plan.

### 203–205 | The earlier authorization and the second launch

**Elena** — “I pulled the old launch record. The warning was still open when we signed. Let’s write down what this flight’s tests actually tell us and which risks we’re prepared to accept.”

Show the following line when a test finds a ground data link fault.

**Elena** — “This test found a fault in the ground data link. I remember losing contact two years ago. For this flight we can compare the remaining downlink capacity with what the sponsor needs.”

Flight review shows any evidence or retesting still required and the residual risks the player can sign for. Once the vehicle is cleared, the player runs the orbital attempt and ground contact. Faults, lift margin, and the delivered data follow the simulation.

A team can hold the countdown. Artemis I once scrubbed a launch attempt after a liquid hydrogen leak. [NASA launch record](https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2022/09/03/artemis-i-launch-attempt-scrubbed/)

### 206–207 | Orbit confirmation and the lunar offer

- Delivery complete. **Samuel** — “We have enough data to verify the orbit. The lunar program can review your record and decide what support to offer.”
- Delivery short. **Elena** — “The demonstration fell short of the contract. The flight record shows how far the vehicle got and what the ground station received.”
- Flight held. The report identifies the blocking conditions. The first successful delivery unlocks the lunar chapter. Unlocked chapters can be replayed.

## Chapter 3 · Lunar Contract

### 301–302 | Surface spectra and the partners’ terms

**Samuel** — “Your orbital demonstration gave our partners a result to review. Now we need spectra from the Moon to choose the next mapping sites. The partners have offers to discuss before you commit the design.”

The player chooses funding, technical support, or an independent plan. The request to advance the deadline, the government’s second payload, and the partner’s live camera appear according to their own conditions. Each event describes the change to schedule, budget, mass, or integration work. Route and payload decisions determine the propellant margin available in flight.

### 303–304 | Flight review through lunar night

Review checks the actual configuration, propellant, any outstanding interface work, and the risks eligible for acceptance. The simulation shows the stages reached, from launch and Earth-orbit checkout through transfer, lunar orbit, descent, first surface day, and lunar night. The player responds using remaining budget, propellant, and surface time. The results preserve these decisions and their consequences.

Peregrine could not carry out its surface mission, but NASA instruments returned some data during flight. [NASA Peregrine mission report](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-science-astrobotic-peregrine-mission-one-concludes/)

### 305 / E01–E03 | Back in the lab

- **Surface delivery complete.** Samuel confirms the mapping team has its spectra. The next team can read the test reports, the risks the team signed for, and the flight record. A new proposal arrives. Alex asks the team to read the requirements together.
- **Flight attempted, delivery short.** Elena accounts for the flight progress and keeps any data recovered. Alex asks the team to review what to change for another attempt.
- **Flight held.** Elena keeps the unresolved conditions and existing evidence. Alex asks the team to plan the next attempt from that record.

The mission portfolio can be expanded to see the best results across the three chapters, data delivered, spending, schedule, configuration, verification checks, and accumulated partnership benefits. Full details of the current attempt stay with its results.
